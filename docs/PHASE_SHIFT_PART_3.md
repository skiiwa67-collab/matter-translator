## The new angle: geometry of the resonance field

Chris noticed that the geometry of the volume itself is a resonance
property, not just the polarity. A square has corners where field lines
bunch and concentrate — stress points that destabilize the resonance.
A circle spreads the field evenly around the boundary, so there is no
weak spot for it to leak or collapse from.

The resonance field couples to the surface, not the volume. So the field
only has to match the outer silhouette — the skin envelope — not the
internal anatomy.

## Curvature math (rough)

- Sphere: uniform curvature everywhere. One clean fundamental mode, no
  preferred direction, nothing to scatter energy into.
- Cube: faces are flat (curvature 0), but twelve edges concentrate
  curvature into sharp points. Those act like antennas leaking resonance
  into higher harmonics. The cube is the worst case — maximum curvature
  concentration per unit volume.
- Human body: irregular but smooth. No sharp angles. Behaves like a
  broadband resonator — stable, forgiving, no single weak frequency to
  collapse. A human may actually be easier to translate than a cube,
  as long as there are no sharp angles.
- Fingertip curvature: ~125 per meter. Smooth egg surface: ~7 per meter.
  Nineteen-to-one spike. Each spike is a leak point.

## The hair problem

A hundred thousand hairs add about 3.3 square meters of surface — nearly
double the body itself (~1.9 m²). If the field has to conform to every
hair, you are not translating a human, you are translating a fractal.

Fix: stop wrapping the human. Translate a bounding volume — an egg, a
capsule, a sphere — and the human is cargo inside it. Same move as the
cube. The cube was never the point; the volume was.

## The capsule

Instead of building a boundary layer on the surface of the human, create
the boundary layer on the surface of a capsule containing what you want
to translate. The capsule is made of an exotic metal or exotic material.

The capsule solves three problems at once:

1. Geometry: one clean, smooth surface. No fingers, no hairs, no
   protrusions. A hard edge instead of a fuzzy one.
2. Energy: a conforming field around a human is roughly 4.8 m² of surface
   to energize. A capsule boundary is about 1.4 m². Roughly a third of
   the energy.
3. Containment: a conforming field has fuzzy edges and leaks to nearby
   objects (the corner of a table). A capsule boundary is a sharp cutoff.
   Nothing outside translates.

Critical rule: the capsule is the field generator, not the cargo. It
stays at A. Only the volume inside translates. If the capsule translated
with the volume, you would be hauling ~14 kg of exotic metal every trip
(1 mm wall at 10 g/cm³). The capsule is the key. The volume inside is the
lock's tumbler. The key does not move. It just fits.

## Contact surfaces — the hard no

If the human stands inside the capsule and touches it, that is a
mechanical coupling between two resonators at different frequencies.
Capsule fundamental ~1645 Hz (exotic metal, speed of sound ~5000 m/s).
Human body resonance ~441 Hz. A contact patch is a bridge. Energy leaks
across it like two tuning forks touching.

It is not about area. Feet on the floor are only ~2.8% of the capsule
surface, but a small contact patch at the right spot can dump the whole
resonance.

The femtosecond window makes it worse. Three femtoseconds is a
trillionth of a second, but even one cycle of a 500 Hz resonance takes
2 milliseconds — roughly 7e11 resonance cycles during a single
translation. A weak bridge over that many cycles is a catastrophic energy
leak.

Conclusion: contact is not a tuning problem. It is a hard no. The cargo
must be suspended.

Suspension options:

1. Levitate the cargo (magnetic, acoustic, electrostatic). Zero contact,
   zero coupling. Cleanest, most complex.
2. Air gap. Impedance mismatch kills most coupling. Simple. A gap of
   roughly a meter (5 wavelengths at 1645 Hz in air) should be safe.
3. Node placement. Touch the capsule only where its standing wave has
   zero displacement. Elegant but fragile — a human shifting weight walks
   off the node and coupling spikes.

Deeper issue: the human inside is also a resonator. Breathing, heartbeat,
thermal radiation, Brownian motion of air molecules — at femtosecond
scales, all of it is an event. This pushes toward a sealed capsule with
vacuum inside and levitated cargo. OR toward the resonance being so
inherently stable that noise does not matter. We do not know which yet.

## Open questions (added 2026-09-26)

- How tight does the bounding volume have to be? Loose = stable but you
  translate empty space. Tight = risk clipping a shoulder or foot.
- Does the resonance couple to the field or to the object? (Still open
  from Part Two, now with geometry as a third variable.)
- How does the capsule's material properties (density, speed of sound,
  conductivity) affect the resonance? What exotic material is right?
- Can the resonance survive the mechanical noise of a living human inside
  (breathing, heartbeat) without a vacuum?
- What is the real math? Still missing pieces. Work with the tools we
  have.
